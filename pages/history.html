🏛️ Chronology of a Classic

The 3,300+ Year History of Tic-Tac-Toe

From stone carvings in pharaonic Egypt and Roman plazas to the dawn of artificial intelligence and Cold War pop culture.

1300 BCE
Ancient Egypt

The Temple of Kurna Carvings

The earliest known 3×3 grid patterns were discovered etched into the roofing slabs of the Temple of Kurna in Thebes, Egypt, dating back to roughly 1300 BCE during the reign of Seti I. Workers and priests played a three-in-a-row alignment game using small stones.

1st Century BCE
Roman Empire

Terni Lapilli (Three Pebbles)

In the Roman Empire, citizens played Terni Lapilli across forums and pavements throughout Rome. Each player had only 3 pebbles (similar to modern Infinite Mode) and moved them between intersections after initial placement.

1952 CE
Cambridge University

OXO — The First AI Video Game

Created by Alexander S. Douglas on the Cambridge EDSAC computer for his PhD thesis, OXO was one of the earliest computer video games in human history. Players used a rotary telephone dial to input moves displayed on a cathode-ray tube screen.

1983 CE
Hollywood Pop Culture

WarGames — "The Only Winning Move"

In the 1983 sci-fi movie WarGames, military supercomputer WOPR plays Tic-Tac-Toe against itself until it learns that every optimal game ends in a tie. It extrapolates this lesson to thermonuclear war, concluding with the iconic quote: "A strange game. The only winning move is not to play."
