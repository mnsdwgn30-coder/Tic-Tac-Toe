The 255,168 Total Game Permutations

When considering all move orders without accounting for symmetry, there are exactly 255,168 unique finished game outcomes:

X Wins: 131,184 (51.4%) Draws: 46,080 (18.1%) O Wins: 77,904 (30.5%)

Notice the clear first-mover advantage for Player X: under completely random play, Player 1 wins over 51% of the time, Player 2 wins 30.5%, and only 18% end in draws. However, with rational agents (Minimax), the draw rate becomes 100%.

🔄 Group Theory

The Dihedral Group (D4) Symmetries

A 3×3 grid possesses 8 geometric symmetries under the Dihedral Group D4:

  • 🔹 4 Rotational states (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°)
  • 🔹 4 Reflections (horizontal, vertical, diagonal, anti-diagonal)

While there are 39 = 19,683 possible cell states, filtering for legal move counts reduces it to 5,478. Applying D4 symmetry reduction collapses all possible game situations down to just 765 distinct strategic boards.

🤖 Minimax Algorithm

Why Perfect Play Always Draws

In Game Theory, Tic-Tac-Toe is classified as a finite, sequential, deterministic, zero-sum game with perfect information.

By Zermelo's Theorem (1913), every such game has a determined game-theoretic value: either Player 1 has a winning strategy, Player 2 has a winning strategy, or both players can force at least a draw.

The Minimax value of the starting board is mathematically 0 (Draw). Neither player can ever force a victory against a rational opponent.

⚡ Minimax Mathematical Formulation

The Minimax Evaluation Function

The Minimax value V(s) of any board state s is computed recursively:

V(s) =
  +10 - depth,   if s is a terminal WIN for Maximizer (AI)
  depth - 10,   if s is a terminal WIN for Minimizer (Opponent)
  0,           if s is a terminal DRAW
  maxa V(result(s, a)),  if turn is Maximizer
  mina V(result(s, a)),  if turn is Minimizer

By appending Alpha-Beta Pruning, branches that cannot possibly influence the final decision are discarded early, reducing computation time by over 80% without losing optimal precision.
